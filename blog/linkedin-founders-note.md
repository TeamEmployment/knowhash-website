Two years ago I got tired of typing the same LinkedIn message with a different name and title every time.

I run an IT recruitment agency here in Australia. We'd already solved this exact problem in our own in-house platform — write a template once, and the right fields fill themselves in for whoever you're messaging next. It saved a genuinely stupid amount of time.

So the obvious question was: why doesn't LinkedIn work like this?

I built a small browser extension that reads whatever profile you're already looking at, and fills your template in for you. No new tab, no CRM, no account to set up. I called it knowhash, because there's no fiddly hash-syntax or curly-brace nonsense to remember — it just reads the page in front of you.

That solved the messaging problem. But it exposed the bigger one underneath it: I wasn't just messaging people on LinkedIn, I was trying to track who I'd spoken to, what they were suited for, and where they'd gotten to — without a second system to keep in sync.

That's knowhash Recruit, and it's live now.

It turns LinkedIn itself into your pipeline:

→ Captures name, headline and company straight off the profile — no re-typing
→ Simple tagging and rating, right where you find the candidate
→ kSIP requests exactly the details and files you need — nothing more
→ Turns a shortlist into a clean, branded summary you can send in one click
→ Still includes the original knowhash messaging — free, and still the same idea that started all this

If you're a recruiter and any of this sounds familiar, I'd genuinely like you to try it and tell me what you think.
