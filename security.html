Most recruiting tools ask you to move your candidate data onto their servers just to use the product. knowhash doesn't. Here's exactly where everything lives, and what protects the one thing that ever leaves your browser.

The backup passphrase

A local-only tool still needs a way to survive a lost laptop or a wiped profile. That's what backups are for — and it's the one moment we ask you to trust a file instead of just your browser, so it's the one moment we lock it properly.

  • Generated for you — seven random words, picked with a cryptographically secure generator the moment you install. Nobody has to invent a good password, which is usually where "secure" passwords fail in practice.
  • Only ever on your machine — we never see it, never store a copy of it, never have a "forgot your passphrase" link to send. There's nothing on our end to leak, because there's nothing on our end.
  • No recovery if it's lost — that's not a missing feature. A recoverable passphrase is a passphrase someone else could also recover. Write it down somewhere that isn't the computer it protects.

What's not encrypted, on purpose

We'd rather tell you this plainly than have you discover it and wonder what else we didn't mention.

  • Candidate packages you generate to share a person's details with a colleague or client aren't encrypted.
  • Spreadsheet exports of your shortlist aren't encrypted either.

Both exist because you chose to hand that data to someone, on purpose, for them to read. At that point it's a document like any other you've ever emailed — yours to manage, not ours to lock. Encrypting a file you're actively trying to share with someone would just mean building a second system to hand them the key, which defeats the point of never running a server in the first place.

Why this matters more than it sounds

Every recruiting platform that stores your candidates on its own servers is a standing liability that exists for as long as your account does — not a one-off risk, a permanent one.
